# Writing your CV

[Back to the user guide](README.md)

## The form

You write your CV in the **Form** on the left. Every field has a short tip:
rest the mouse pointer on it to read what to write.

From the top, the form has:

1. **The job ad you are applying to** – optional. If you are applying for a
   particular job, paste the ad here first. It is not printed in the CV; it
   helps CVBuilder fill in the job title and find the keywords. Without an ad
   the card is one line, so a new CV starts with your name. See [Importing an old CV and using the job ad](import-and-job-ads.md).
2. **Contact details** – your name, headline, contact line and photo.
3. **The CV's sections** – in the same order as in the CV.

### Contact details

- **Full name** – as you want to be addressed. It stands at the top of the CV.
- **Professional headline** – one short line under your name. Use the job
  title from the ad if it is true for you, for example
  "Nurse | Emergency department | Medication management".
- **Contact line** – email, phone, city and country, and LinkedIn.
  - Write the phone number with the country code, for example `+47 412 34 567`.
  - LinkedIn must be the full address, starting with `https://`.
  - The arrows move a line up or down. The CV shows them in this order.
  - **+ Add contact field** adds a line of your own, for example GitHub,
    a portfolio or a driving licence.
  - **Recommended order** puts the lines back in the usual order.

### The sections

The standard sections are:

- **Profile** – a short text, 30–70 words: who you are, what you are best at,
  and what you want to do in this job.
- **Core skills** – one skill or tool per row. The **Group** box collects skills
  under a heading, for example "Tools". Use the job ad's own words where they
  are true.
- **Work experience** – one card per job (see below).
- **Education** – qualification, school, and start and end year.
- **Courses and certifications** – name, issuer and year.
- **Languages** – the language and your level, for example "English – fluent"
  or "German – B2".
- **Interests** – optional. Tick at most three to show in the CV. Untick
  **Include interests in the CV** to leave them out.

**Courses and certifications**, **Languages** and **Interests** start folded.
Click a section's title to open or fold it. A folded section is still in the CV.

Buttons on each section's header:

- **Up / down arrows** – move the section in the CV.
- **Colour button** – give the section its own colour (see
  [Designs and colours](designs-and-colours.md)).
- **Bin** – delete the section (only for sections you added yourself).

**Recommended order** at the bottom of the form puts the sections back in the
order recruiters expect: profile, skills, experience, education, and so on.

### A job

For each job, fill in:

- **Job title** and **Employer**.
- **Location · city** (optional, but many systems sort by place).
- **From** and **To** dates. You can type `09.2021`, `9/2021`, `2021-09`,
  `sep. 2021`, or just a year such as `2010`.
- Tick **Current job** if you still work there. You can also type "now" in the
  **To** box.
- **What you did and achieved** – one bullet point per line. Aim for 2–4 short
  points per job. Start with a verb and show the result, for example
  "Cut waiting times by 20% by introducing …".

If a date cannot be read, a red note under the box tells you what to type.

### Adding things

Use the **Insert** tab, or the **+ Add …** buttons at the bottom of each section:

- **Experience** group: **Job**, **Education**, **Course**, and **Section**.
- **Skills** group: **Skill**, **Language**, **Interest**.
- **Contact** group: **Contact field** and **Photo**.

## Your own sections

You can add sections such as Projects, Volunteering, Positions of trust,
Publications, Awards, Talks and presentations, References, Military service,
Patents or Other experience.

1. Choose **Insert → Section** and pick a type, or **Custom section with its
   own name**. You can also click **+ Add custom section** at the bottom of the
   form.
2. In the **New section** window, type the name as it should stand in the CV.
3. Under **Position in the CV**, choose where it goes: at the top, at the
   bottom, or after a given section.
4. Click **Add**.

Each entry in your own section has a **Title**, an **Organization or place**,
a free **Time** text (for example "2021–2023"), and bullet points. Use
**+ Add entry** for more entries. Delete the whole section with the bin on its
header.

## One full CV, tailored copies

Keep everything you have done in one full CV, and leave out what a particular
job does not need, without deleting it.

- The **eye** button on a job, an education, a skill, a language, a course or
  an entry of your own leaves it out of this CV. Click it again to show it.
- Under a job's bullet points, **Bullets in this CV** lists them with a tick
  each: untick the ones this CV should not have.
- What is hidden stays in the form (greyed, folded to its title) and in the
  file. It is left out of the preview, the PDF and Word file, the copied text,
  the keyword match, the checks and the page count. A line at the top of the
  form says how many items are hidden, with **Show everything again**, and the
  status bar shows the count too. Untick **Show hidden items in the form** to
  see only what is in the CV.
- **File → Save tailored copy …** saves this CV, with what you have hidden, the
  ad and the keywords, as a copy for the job (named after the employer). The
  copy is the open CV from then on; the full CV stays as it is in its own file.
  The line at the top of a copy says which CV it was made from, and
  **Open the full CV** opens it.
- The AI can do this too: ask it to bring the CV down to two pages for a job,
  and it hides the least relevant points instead of deleting them.

CV files are now saved in a newer format (version 3) that keeps track of each
item. CVBuilder versions from before this feature cannot open them; they say
the file was saved by a newer CVBuilder.

## The live preview

The middle of the window shows the real pages, laid out exactly as the PDF and
Word file will be. The preview updates a moment after you stop typing.

- If the CV gets longer than recruiters advise, a note above the pages says so.
  One page is advised for a short career (at most two jobs and under about three
  years), two pages for most people. There is no hard limit.
- **View → Zoom** sets the size: **Fit** (fills the width), 75 %, 100 % (real
  size) or 150 %.
- **View → Views** shows or hides the **Form**, the **Preview** and the
  **AI and checklist** panel. **Show all** brings them all back.
- You can drag the edges between the panels to make them wider or narrower.

## The example and a new CV

- **File → New CV** starts an empty CV. It keeps the design and colours you
  chose, and starts in your CV language. The CV you had before can be brought
  back with **Undo**.
- **File → Open example** shows the made-up example again. Your own CV can be
  brought back with **Undo**.

## Photo

A photo is optional. It is common in some countries (for example Norway) and
not expected in others.

1. Click the empty frame next to your name (**Add photo**), or use
   **Insert → Photo**.
2. Choose a picture (JPG, PNG, WebP, BMP or TIFF).
3. In **Adjust the portrait**, use the sliders: **Zoom**, **Left–right**,
   **Up–down**, **Straighten (degrees)**, **Brightness** and **Contrast**.
   **Reset** starts over.
4. Click **Use photo**.

Good to know:

- Your original picture file is never changed.
- CVBuilder keeps a small copy, cropped to a portrait shape. Hidden data such
  as the camera's GPS location is left out.
- **Show in CV** lets you keep the photo in your draft without printing it.
- **Remove photo** deletes it from the draft.
- Click the photo to change it.

## Undo

CVBuilder has an **Undo** for big changes. It brings back the CV as it was
before the last big change, for example:

- starting a new CV or opening the example,
- opening or importing a CV,
- filling in from a job ad,
- deleting a job or one of your own sections,
- switching the CV's language.

You find **Undo** on the **Home** tab and in the status bar at the bottom.

Changes made by the AI assistant have their own undo button in the chat. See
[The AI assistant](ai-assistant.md#seeing-and-undoing-changes).

When a new CV, the example or an imported CV replaces your own, CVBuilder also
keeps a backup copy of the one you had in its data folder.

## Saving

### Save and Save as

- **File → Save** or **Ctrl+S** (Cmd+S on a Mac) saves the CV to its file.
  The first time, you choose where to save it and what to call it.
- **File → Save as** saves a copy under a new name or in a new place. From
  then on, **Save** uses the new file.
- The file is a CVBuilder draft (`.json`). Open it again with **File → Open**.

The file contains your personal details. Keep it somewhere safe, and think
twice before you share it.

To share your CV with an employer, download a PDF or Word file instead. See
[The checklist and downloading](checklist-and-export.md).

### Autosave every 5 minutes

Once your CV has a file, CVBuilder saves your changes to that file every five
minutes, like Word does. The status bar then says "Saved automatically to …".

You can turn this off in **File → Settings → Draft →
Save automatically every 5 minutes**.

### Remembered draft

Separately from your file, CVBuilder remembers your draft on this computer as you
type, while **Remember the draft on this computer** is ticked. If you open
CVBuilder again, it says "Welcome back" and your CV is there.

On a shared computer, untick this box and save your CV as a file before you
close.

### The reminder when you close

If you close CVBuilder and the CV has changes that are not in its file yet,
CVBuilder asks **Do you want to save the changes to your CV?**

- **Save** – save the CV and close.
- **Don't save** – close without saving to the file.
- **Cancel** – go back to your CV.

The example CV never asks.
